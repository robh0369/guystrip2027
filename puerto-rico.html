The Big Night

Free · Friday, late

There's no ballgame to build the weekend around, so the anchor is La Placita de Santurce — a produce market by day that turns into about twenty bars around one plaza after dark, with the drinking happening in the street between them. No tickets, no covers, no reservations. Friday is the night.

How La Placita actually works

It's a working market building in the middle of a plaza in Santurce. During the day people buy plantains there. From about 6pm Wednesday through Sunday the surrounding bars open, and by 10 the streets around the plaza are closed to cars and packed.

Nobody sits down. You buy a drink at a window, stand in the street, and move when you feel like it. That's the whole format, and it's the best possible setup for six people who can't agree on anything.

It's a 10-minute Uber from Condado and about 15 from Old San Juan. Going home late is the one time the whole group should split two cars rather than wait for a six-seater.

San Juan is a thin strip of city between the Atlantic and a set of lagoons, which is why everything lines up east to west. Old San Juan sits on its own islet at the far end; we'd stay in the middle, in Condado. The Map button in the top bar pulls this up from anywhere on the page.

Flights

Chicago direct · Cincinnati connects

This is the one place San Juan is harder than Boston. Chicago has plenty of nonstops. Cincinnati has none at all, so the Cincinnati crew is connecting no matter what.

How to land together

Try to put everyone on the same ORD–SJU nonstop. If the Cincinnati crew connects through Chicago, the whole group walks off one plane in San Juan. That's worth an early alarm and a tight-ish layover.

Aim for a 3:00 to 6:00pm Thursday arrival. SJU is about 20 minutes from Condado, so a 5pm landing still makes an 8pm table in Old San Juan comfortably.

Nothing to do on arrival. It's a domestic flight into a US territory: no passports, no customs, no currency, and phones work normally. Bags come out and you're in an Uber.

Sunday home: late afternoon or evening. Same as the Boston plan, and it leaves Sunday morning free for a swim.

Where We're Staying

No hotels · Registration rules

A house for the six of us, same as Boston. Puerto Rico has its own licensing regime for short-term rentals, and San Juan added a layer on top, so the rule below is worth two minutes before anyone books.

The one rule that matters

Every legal short-term rental in Puerto Rico is registered with the Puerto Rico Tourism Company as an innkeeper (hostelero), and the PRTC number is supposed to appear on the listing. On top of that, San Juan's Ordinance 39 requires an annual municipal licence for every unit inside city limits.

The check is the same as it was in Boston: look for the registration number on the listing page. If it isn't there, ask the host. A host who can't produce one is either unlicensed or evasive, and both are reasons to move on.

There's also a 7% room occupancy tax remitted to the PRTC. A legitimate listing will show it in the price breakdown rather than asking for cash on arrival.

Worth knowing: unlike Boston, Puerto Rico does not require the owner to live there, so legal inventory is far deeper than it was in the North End. This is a paperwork check, not a scavenger hunt.

Which neighborhood

Condado is the pick. It's the only one of the three that's a walk to a swimmable beach, a walk to dinner, and a short ride to both Old San Juan and La Placita. Old San Juan is the more romantic choice and the worse base.

Wherever it lands, get the van's parking sorted before Saturday. Condado and Ocean Park houses often come with a spot or a driveway. Inside the old city, they don't.

The Weekend

Four days

One main thing per day, same as Boston. Friday is the long walk and the big night, Saturday is the water, and the van only exists for one of the four days. Every place below links out to a map and hours.

Day 1 · Thursday

20 May: Get in, eat well

Everyone lands at different times, so night one stays easy. Drop bags, walk the old city at golden hour, get a big table.
Afternoon
Land at SJU. It's about 20 minutes from Condado and there's no customs line, because it isn't international. Uber works normally here and is cheap. Whoever gets in first grabs the dinner table.
6:00p
Into Old San Juan and walk the Paseo de la Princesa out to the fountain, then up Calle Fortaleza. Flat, about a mile all in, and the light on the blue cobblestones at that hour is the whole reason to do it first.
7:30p
Dinner in the old city. Marmalade is the nicer one and takes reservations; Café El Punto and Deaverdura are the honest criollo options at half the price. Order mofongo once as a group so everyone can say they did, then order what you actually want.
9:30p
La Factoría on Calle San Sebastián. No sign on the door, six connected bars inside, each with its own menu and its own music, and you drift between them through unmarked doorways. It was named the best bar in the Caribbean in 2026. Go early-ish, because the line after 11 is real.
11:30p
The rest of Calle San Sebastián is four solid blocks of bars, so the night ends whenever it ends and the Uber home is fifteen minutes.
Day 2 · Friday

21 May: The forts, then La Placita

Five hundred years of Spanish military engineering in the morning, a rum distillery after lunch, and the loudest plaza in the Caribbean at night.
Big night
9:00a
Castillo San Felipe del Morro the moment it opens, before the cruise crowd and the heat. Six levels of gun batteries on a headland, with the lighthouse from the top of this page. $10 covers both forts for 24 hours, so keep the receipt. Under-16s are free.
10:30a
Walk the Paseo del Morro, the paved path along the base of the city wall at the water's edge, then back up through the Santa María Magdalena cemetery and Cuartel de Ballajá. This is the Freedom Trail equivalent: about two and a half miles, flat, and you can bail into a bar at any point.
12:30p
Castillo San Cristóbal at the other end of the old city — bigger than El Morro, far emptier, and the tunnels are the best part. Same ticket.
2:30p
Walk down to Pier 2 and take the AcuaExpreso ferry across the bay to Cataño for Casa Bacardí. The ferry is about $1.50 round trip and takes 12 minutes. The distillery tour is $40–45; the mixology class is $89 and better if the group's into it.
6:30p
Back across, clean up, and eat something real before the plaza. Santaella is a hundred yards from La Placita and exactly the right call, so book it.
9:30p
La Placita de Santurce. Don't arrive early. The plaza fills around 10 and the streets close to traffic after that. Live salsa in several of the bars. Jungle Bird is two blocks away if anyone wants a proper cocktail and a seat.
Late
It runs until 3 or later, and nobody has to drive. Split into two Ubers going home instead of waiting twenty minutes for one six-seater.
Day 3 · Saturday

22 May: The van day east

Rainforest before it gets hot, a catamaran to a deserted island in the afternoon, and roadside fritters on the drive home.
Water day
7:15a
Van out of Condado. This is the one early start of the trip and the only day we need a vehicle. An eight-seater from SJU or a Condado agency runs about $120–150 for the day. Split six ways it's cheaper than paying for six tour pickups.
8:00a
El Yunque, the only tropical rainforest in the US Forest Service system. Free, no reservation needed, gates open at 8. Do the Yokahú tower first for the view, then the Angelito trail down to the swimming hole on the Río Mameyes. La Mina Falls, Big Tree and Baño de Oro are all closed, so ignore every blog that sends you there.
11:30a
Back in the van, 30 minutes east to Villa Marina in Fajardo. Check-in is at noon.
1:00p
Catamaran to Cayo Icacos, an uninhabited island off Fajardo. Four hours, back at the dock at 5. Lunch, snorkel gear, kayaks and tropical drinks are all included. Details in the box below.
5:45p
Stop at the Luquillo kiosks on the way back — about sixty family-run stalls in a row along PR-3, right by the beach. Alcapurrias, bacalaítos, whole fried snapper, and a beer for three dollars. This is the best food of the weekend and it costs nothing.
8:00p
Back in San Juan. Late, low-key dinner on Calle Loíza, which is a ten-minute walk from Condado and where people actually live.
The catamaran, for anyone who hasn't done one

Same idea as the Boston sail, warmer water. You buy a ticket like a movie, show up at the marina fifteen minutes early, and step onto a 50-foot catamaran with a crew of three. It motors 45 minutes out to a reef, everyone snorkels, then it anchors off a sand beach with nothing on it and stays a couple of hours.

The 1:00pm departure is the one that makes Saturday work, because it leaves the whole morning for the rainforest. Most operators only push the 8:00am trip, so book the afternoon deliberately.

Heads up: the bio bay won't work this weekend

Fajardo's bioluminescent lagoon — Laguna Grande, where the water lights up when you move it — is the obvious thing to add to a day already spent in Fajardo. It's also the one thing the calendar takes away from us.

20 May 2027 is a full moon. The glow is caused by microscopic plankton, and it only reads against a genuinely dark sky. Thursday is the full moon, and Friday and Saturday are barely better, with a bright waning moon up most of the night. Every operator will still happily sell you a ticket.

If someone wants to go anyway, it's an $85–125 paddle through a mangrove channel that's worth doing on its own terms — just don't expect the photos. The new moon was 6 May. If the group would rather have the bio bay than the Grand Prix weekend in Montréal, moving the whole trip two weeks earlier is the move, and it's worth saying out loud now rather than in April.

Day 4 · Sunday

23 May: One last swim, then home

The only morning with nothing scheduled. Walk to the water, eat a long breakfast, go to the airport.
9:00a
Balneario El Escambrón, a protected cove tucked under the old city walls with a reef close enough to swim to. It's the best snorkeling inside city limits and it's free. Fifteen minutes on foot from Condado along the water, or a four-dollar Uber. Lifeguards, showers, and a kiosk for coffee.
11:00a
Breakfast on Calle Loíza or in Ocean Park. Both are a walk rather than a ride, and Sunday is when the neighborhood is out.
1:00p
Optional: if the flights are late enough, Piñones is 15 minutes east — a road through the mangroves lined with open-air kiosks, with a bike path along the dunes.
Mid afternoon
Uber to SJU. About 20 minutes from Condado, and the terminal is small enough that 90 minutes is plenty.

If Something Goes Sideways

Backups
If the trade winds kill the boat
May can blow hard enough that Icacos trips get cancelled the morning of. Drive 20 minutes further to Balneario Seven Seas instead — a sheltered bay with calm water, shade and a walk out to hidden coves. Costs $6 to park and nothing else.
If it rains all Saturday
Go anyway. El Yunque is a rainforest and it rains there most days; that's the point, and the swimming hole doesn't care. If it's genuinely torrential, the fallback is the Museo de Arte de Puerto Rico, the Casa Bacardí mixology class, and the forts, which are half indoors.
If someone wants a real hike
Mount Britton tower and up Forest Road 10 toward El Yunque Peak is a proper climb with real elevation, and it's the one trail system in the park still fully open. Add 90 minutes to Saturday morning and start at 7:00 instead of 7:15.
If the group wants the bio bay
Then the dates have to move. The new moon is 6 May 2027, so a Thu 6 – Sun 9 May version of this exact trip gets a dark sky, dodges the Grand Prix problem in Montréal entirely, and changes nothing else on this page. Worth a vote before anyone books flights.
If sargassum shows up early
The seaweed season usually starts late May and mostly spares the north-facing city beaches. If Condado looks rough, Escambrón is protected by its reef and stays clean when the open beaches don't.
