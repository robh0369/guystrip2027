Race Week, No Ticket

Free · Thu–Sun, 11a–11p

Formula 1 published its 2027 calendar and the Canadian Grand Prix lands on 21–23 May at Circuit Gilles-Villeneuve — the same three days we'd already picked. That's either the best coincidence of the trip or an expensive accident, depending on whether you buy a ticket. The plan is not to.

Heads up: this is the expensive weekend

Skipping the race doesn't get us out of race-week pricing. Grand Prix weekend is the single biggest tourist event in Canada. Hotels run about 95% full and mid-range rooms have gone for $600–900 a night. That, not the racing, is the real cost of these dates, and it's why the lodging section below looks nothing like Boston's.

The 2027 festival dates aren't posted yet. Crescent Street has run Thursday through Sunday of race week for 25 years, and with a Sunday 23 May race that should mean 20–23 May — exactly our weekend. Worth confirming on crescentgrandprix.com before anyone buys a plane ticket, the same way we'd check a first pitch.

If the group would rather not do race week at all, everything else on this page works untouched on a quieter May weekend, and the lodging gets roughly $100 a head cheaper. That's a real vote to take.

The Lay of the Land

Pins match the day colors

Montréal is an island in the middle of the St Lawrence with a small mountain in the middle of it. Downtown sits between the mountain and the river, the old city is on the waterfront, and the circuit is on a man-made island just offshore. Almost all of it is walkable, and the rest is two métro stops.

The one thing this trip needs that the others don't

Six valid passports. This is an international trip and there's no way around it. A passport card is not enough for air travel — it has to be the book. Renewals have run 6 to 10 weeks, so anyone whose passport expires before November 2027 should check the date this week, not in April.

Book race weekend early or not at all. Grand Prix flights into YUL fill and climb. If the group commits to these dates, flights want booking about the same time as the rooms, not after.

Nothing else is different. Canada takes cards everywhere, US phone plans mostly roam fine, and the border at YUL for six Americans on a weekend trip is a two-minute conversation. Bring the passport, not a story.

Sunday home: evening. The race finishes mid-afternoon and the city empties into YUL right after, so either fly out before the traffic or well after it.

Where We're Staying

The house plan doesn't work here

This is the one section where Montréal breaks the format. The "one house, one table" plan that worked in Boston and works in San Juan is essentially illegal here in May, and it's worth understanding why before anyone sends around an Airbnb link.

The one rule that matters

Montréal only allows short-term rentals in residential areas between 10 June and 10 September. Our dates are in May. That means almost every "whole home for six" listing you'll find for this weekend is either outside the legal window, on one of a handful of specifically zoned commercial streets, or simply not legal.

Québec also requires every short-term rental to carry a CITQ registration number displayed on the listing, and the city adds its own municipal permit on top. As of September 2026 hosts must also renew written consent from the building owner or condo board every year or the registration lapses.

The practical upshot: an unlicensed listing can be pulled days before check-in, and on the busiest weekend of the Canadian year there is no backup inventory to move into. That's a much worse failure mode than Boston's.

So the plan here is rooms, not a house — but there's a way to do that without it feeling like a business trip. The three options below are all legal year-round.

Three routes to six beds downtown on race weekend. All three are walking distance to Crescent Street.

Option 1, the budget play

University residences

C$65–180 / room / night

McGill's summer residences open around 15 May. La Citadelle on Sherbrooke has private rooms with private bathrooms and A/C, four blocks from Crescent Street. Concordia's Grey Nuns is a converted convent and starts near C$65. Legal, central, and immune to race-week pricing.

Option 2, closest to a house

Aparthotel suites

C$400–900 / night

Licensed apartment hotels with full kitchens and multi-bedroom units — the nearest thing to the Boston plan that's legal in May. Eurostars Old Montreal Suites, Maison Rasco and ApartHotelMontreal all have units that sleep six to eight. Expect race-week rates, and book these first.

Option 3, the pressure valve

Off-core hotel rooms

C$250–450 / room / night

Three doubles in the Plateau, Griffintown, Verdun, or across the river in Longueuil — one stop on the Yellow Line and materially cheaper than downtown on this weekend. You give up the five-minute stumble home from Crescent Street; the métro runs until 1am and Ubers exist.

Which one

Price the aparthotel first, then decide. If a six-sleeper suite comes in near C$600 a night, it's the best version of this trip and it keeps the one-table feeling. If race week pushes it past C$900, the McGill residences are genuinely good, genuinely central, and save something like $150 a head — which is the difference between this trip landing inside the Boston budget and blowing past it.

Whatever we pick, book it before the flights. Rooms are the scarce thing on this weekend, not seats.

The Weekend

Four days

One main thing per day. Friday is the long walk and the big night, Saturday is the water, and nothing on this page needs a car — a three-day transit pass is C$21.75 and covers the airport bus both ways.

Day 1 · Thursday

20 May: Get in, eat well

Everyone lands at different times, so night one stays easy. Drop bags, walk the old city, get a big table.
Afternoon
Land at YUL. The 747 bus runs 24 hours to downtown in about 45 minutes and is included in the three-day pass. Buy the C$21.75 three-day pass at the airport kiosk on arrival. It covers métro, bus and this shuttle, and it's the last transport decision anyone has to make.
6:00p
Drop bags and walk down into Vieux-Montréal — Place Jacques-Cartier, along Rue Saint-Paul, out to the Old Port waterfront. Cobblestones, 18th-century stone warehouses, and terrasses on every corner. It's about 20 minutes downhill from downtown.
7:30p
Dinner in the old city. Garde Manger is the one to try for — a small seafood-forward room with a chalkboard menu that changes daily. Modavie on Saint-Paul is the reliable fallback and has live jazz most nights.
9:30p
AURA at Notre-Dame Basilica — a 30-minute projection-mapped light and orchestral show inside the basilica, which is one of the most ornate interiors in North America to begin with. About $40. Skeptics get won over by this one. If the group would rather drink, the terrasses on Saint-Paul are fifty yards away.
11:00p
Back uphill for one on Crescent Street, which will already be set up for the festival. A look at what Friday's going to be.
Day 2 · Friday

21 May: The mountain, then Crescent Street

Up the mountain in the morning, a bagel-and-smoked-meat crawl down the other side, and a closed-off street full of F1 cars at night.
Big night
9:30a
Walk up Rue Peel to the Kondiaronk Belvedere on Mount Royal — the view at the top of this page. It's a real climb of about 700 steps or a gentler switchback path, your choice, and it takes 40 minutes. Free, and the park is genuinely huge. Anyone who'd rather not climb can take the 11 bus to the same spot.
11:30a
Down the north side into the Plateau — outdoor staircases, painted triplexes, and murals. Keep going to Mile End for a bagel at St-Viateur or Fairmount, wood-fired and open 24 hours. Pick one and argue about it. The whole walk from the summit is about three flat miles.
1:00p
Lunch at Schwartz's on Saint-Laurent. Smoked meat on rye, medium fat, with a pickle and a cherry cola. Cash-friendly, no reservations, communal tables. The line looks bad and isn't — it turns over fast. Next door does takeout if the group would rather eat in the park.
3:00p
Wander Boulevard Saint-Laurent back toward downtown, with a stop at Dieu du Ciel if anyone wants the best brewery in the city.
6:00p
Crescent Street. Four blocks closed to traffic, F1 cars and team displays on the pavement, live bands, and every bar on the street running an outdoor bar. Free and open 11am to 11pm. It gets genuinely crowded after 8, which is the point.
9:00p
Eat without leaving. The Crescent and de la Montagne restaurants all have terrasses open onto the festival, so dinner is wherever there's a table. If the group wants a plan instead: Bar George is two blocks off the noise in a Victorian mansion, and takes reservations.
Late
Crescent runs till 11, then the night moves to Saint-Denis or Saint-Laurent, where last call is 3am. Poutine at La Banquise is open 24 hours and is the correct end to this day.
Day 3 · Saturday

22 May: Out on the water

Slow morning, an hour getting soaked in class-three rapids, a floating spa to recover, and the best dinner of the trip.
Water day
10:00a
Late breakfast, nothing scheduled. Qualifying is on TV around 2pm if anyone cares.
11:30a
Walk the Lachine Canal path from Atwater Market down to the Old Port. Flat, right on the water, about three miles, and lined with the industrial buildings that made the city. Bixi bikes are all along it if three miles sounds like a lot.
2:00p
Saute-Moutons jet boat from the Clock Tower Quay. One hour: 15 minutes out, 30 minutes in the Lachine Rapids, 15 minutes back. About $64. The season opens 15 May, so our weekend is live. Details below.
3:30p
Bota Bota, a spa built into a converted ferry moored in the Old Port, two minutes' walk from the dock. Hot baths, cold plunges, saunas, and a silent deck looking at the river. $70–95 for three hours. Going straight from the rapids into this is the single best sequence on any of the three itineraries.
7:30p
The big dinner. Joe Beef in Little Burgundy is the famous one — six is the maximum they'll take online, so it's exactly our size. Au Pied de Cochon in the Plateau is the other answer and the more excessive one. Book this two months out. Both are Michelin-listed and both are small.
10:30p
Back to Crescent for the last night of the festival, or Saint-Laurent if everyone's had enough of racing.
The jet boat, for anyone who hasn't done one

Simpler than it sounds and not remotely dangerous. You buy a ticket like a movie, show up at the Clock Tower Quay half an hour early, and they hand you a poncho and a life jacket. The boat runs upriver to the Lachine Rapids — standing waves in the St Lawrence big enough that they stopped shipping here for 300 years until they dug the canal — and then spends half an hour driving into them on purpose.

You will get soaked to the skin. That's the product. Leave phones and wallets in the lockers they provide, wear something that dries, and book Bota Bota for straight afterwards.

Day 4 · Sunday

23 May: Race day, from a terrasse

A market, a long brunch, and the Grand Prix on a patio TV with the actual cars audible across the river. Flights are evening.
9:30a
Jean-Talon Market in Little Italy, four métro stops up the Orange Line. Open-air, enormous, and on a Sunday morning it's the whole neighborhood doing its shopping. Eat your way through it rather than sitting down — cheese, charcuterie, oysters, and a cortado at the far end.
11:30a
Brunch in Mile End on the walk back down, or on Saint-Denis. Montréal takes brunch more seriously than it should and the lines prove it.
2:00p
The race, from a patio. First-class engine noise carries clear across the river from Île Notre-Dame, and every terrasse in the city has it on. Find one on Saint-Denis with a TV and sit outside. Roughly two hours. It's a better way to watch a Grand Prix than standing at a fence, and it costs the price of a beer.
Late afternoon
The 747 back to YUL, about 45 minutes and already paid for. The city empties toward the airport right after the race, so leave a buffer or wait it out over one more drink.
One thing not to plan for Sunday

Parc Jean-Drapeau is the racetrack. The island park with the beach, the Biosphère and the Piknic Électronik party is inside the circuit fence on race weekend — no ticket, no entry. Piknic doesn't run, and the Yellow Line métro to Jean-Drapeau will be shoulder-to-shoulder with 100,000 ticket holders.

Every guide to Montréal will recommend that island. On these three days it's off the board, which is exactly why Sunday points north to the market instead.

If Something Goes Sideways

Backups
If the rooms price out of reach
Move the weekend. Everything on this page except the Crescent Street festival works identically on any May weekend, and off race week the same beds are roughly $100 a head cheaper. The anchor event would become the mountain and the rapids, which is a perfectly good trip.
If it rains all Saturday
The jet boat runs in the rain — you're getting soaked regardless — but it does cancel for weather. If it does, Bota Bota is indoors and better in bad weather, the Museum of Fine Arts is free-ish and enormous, and the Underground City means you can cross downtown without going outside.
If someone has to hear the cars
Buy one Friday general admission ticket rather than a weekend pass. Friday practice is a fraction of the price, the circuit is 15 minutes from downtown on the Yellow Line, and it doesn't wreck anyone else's day — Friday's plan is downtown anyway and they can rejoin at Crescent Street.
If someone wants real elevation
Mount Royal's full summit loop from Parc Jeanne-Mance adds a couple of miles and about 700 feet, and there's a second lookout on the east side over the Olympic Stadium that almost nobody walks to. No car, no ticket, still inside the city.
If a passport doesn't come through
This is the failure mode with no workaround, which is why it's first on the booking list. If one guy can't make it international, San Juan is the same weekend, the same budget, and needs nothing but a driver's licence.
